Deviation Reporting Axis — Help
What angle should I enter?
Enter the azimuth (compass bearing) of your structure's main axis, measured clockwise from TRUE North. Range 0–360° (0° = North, 90° = East, 180° = South, 270° = West).
What you will get
  • Along-axis — movement in the direction of the axis you enter (e.g. lengthwise along a dam crest, rail, wall, or slope).
  • Transverse-axis — movement perpendicular to it (90° clockwise), usually the critical direction (e.g. across a dam: upstream/downstream).
How to obtain the angle
  • Design drawing / site plan: read the bearing of the structure's main axis.
  • Map (e.g. Google Earth): draw a line along the axis and read its bearing.
  • Two survey points on the axis: azimuth = atan2(ΔEast, ΔNorth), converted to 0–360°.
  • Compass: read the magnetic bearing, then add the local magnetic declination to get the TRUE bearing.
Example
A dam running North-East to South-West → enter 45. “Along” then reports movement along the dam, and “Transverse” reports movement across it.
Leave the checkbox unticked to report standard East / North / Up. The total movement magnitude is identical in either mode — only the axis direction changes.
Distance from Fixed Bearing — Help
What is a fixed bearing?
A bridge deck lengthens when it warms up and shortens when it cools. To let it move safely, the deck sits on bearings. One support is the fixed bearing: it is pinned and does not let the deck slide along its length. The other supports are expansion (free / sliding) bearings, which let the deck slide. So the deck always grows and shrinks away from the fixed bearing.
Why the distance matters
Thermal movement increases with distance from the fixed bearing. At the fixed bearing it is zero; at the far end it is largest:
ΔL = α × L × (T − Tref)
where α is the Structure Thermal Coefficient, L is the distance you enter here, T is the current temperature and Tref is the Reference Temperature. The correction removes this temperature movement, so the reported deviation shows only real structural movement. An inaccurate L leaves part of the temperature movement in the result.
What to measure
  • The horizontal distance along the deck's length (the longitudinal axis), in metres.
  • From the centre of the fixed bearing to the vertical axis of the GNSS rover antenna.
  • Measure within the same continuous deck unit. If expansion joints split the bridge into separate units, use the fixed bearing of the unit the rover sits on.
How to identify the fixed bearing
  • Bearing schedule / general arrangement drawing: it is marked “Fixed”, “F” or “Pinned”. Expansion bearings are marked “Free”, “Guided” or “E”.
  • On site: expansion bearings have a sliding plate (often stainless steel with PTFE) or slotted holes and a visible movement gap. A fixed bearing has neither.
  • The expansion joint is normally at the end away from the fixed bearing.
How to obtain the distance
  • Drawings: add up the span lengths from the fixed bearing to the antenna position.
  • Site measurement: tape or total station along the deck.
  • Survey coordinates: horizontal distance between the fixed bearing and the antenna, measured along the deck axis.
Direction from Fixed Bearing to Rover
The distance gives the size of the thermal movement; the direction tells which way it pushes the rover. Enter the compass bearing you would face standing at the fixed bearing, looking toward the rover, measured clockwise from TRUE North (0° = North, 90° = East, 180° = South, 270° = West).
[FIXED]======(rover)======[free] rover is EAST of fixed bearing → enter 90 [free]======(rover)======[FIXED] rover is WEST of fixed bearing → enter 270
Getting this the wrong way round reverses the correction and doubles the thermal error instead of removing it. Read it from the site plan the same way as the Reference Axis angle.
Reference Temperature Source
  • Manual — you enter Tref. For correct results this should be the average temperature while the Base Reading (baseline) was being taken; any difference appears as a constant offset of α × L × (Tref − baseline temperature).
  • Automatic — the average temperature over the baseline period is recorded when the baseline is established and used as Tref, so the corrected deviation starts at zero with no offset.
What gets corrected
Only the horizontal deviation, along the bridge: it is split into North and East using the direction above (or applied to Along when the Deviation Reporting Axis is on). The Altitude / Up value is not corrected — this model describes the deck lengthening, not vertical movement. The averaged temperature (AVG_TEMPERATURE) is used.
Example
Steel/concrete deck, α = 12 PPM/°C, rover 40 m from the fixed bearing, temperature 20 °C above the reference: ΔL = 12×10−6 × 40 × 20 = 9.6 mm of movement that is purely thermal. A 1 m error in L changes this by 0.24 mm.
If the rover is mounted on a pier or abutment rather than on the deck, the deck's thermal movement does not apply to it — leave temperature correction disabled.
